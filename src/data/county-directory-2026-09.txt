                             Medina County Flag
      Formed in 1818, Medina County adopted its own flag in 1990. The
county commissioners at the time — John H. Happ, Ralph A. Berry Jr., and
Charles E. Hawley — sponsored a flag design contest in cooperation with
the Medina County Historical Society, the Medina County Gazette, the Old
Phoenix National Bank, and the Medina County School districts.
      The winning design was submitted by Calvin A. Ganyard, a lifelong
resident of the county. The flag was officially adopted on July 30, 1990.
      A 1942 graduate of Medina High School, Ganyard, retired as an
administrative assistant in the Medina County Engineer’s Department in 1989.
      He was motivated to design the flag partly because of his many years
as a county employee, and partly because of his family’s long, distinguished
history. His great-great-grandfather James Ganyard was one of four Ontario,
N.Y. residents who purchased Granger Township when it was first drawn on a
surveyor’s map in 1817.
      Fetus Ganyard was one of the township’s first three trustees; Milton
Ganyard was a Medina County Commissioner in 1880; another Calvin Ganyard
was elected to the Ohio House of Representatives in 1892; and Lester Ganyard
was the Medina County surveyor from 1894 to 1900, a position that preceded
the election of the first county engineer.
Sources: Commissioners’ Records • The Post Newspapers; Glenn Wojciak, staff writer; June 21, 2011; updated September 25, 2012
